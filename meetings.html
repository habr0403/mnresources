Fast guidance

How to pick your first meeting

  1. Start with identification. Choose a group where the problem sounds like yours.
  2. Choose a philosophy. Spiritual, secular, Buddhist, Christian, Indigenous, medication-friendly, or pathway-neutral.
  3. Check eligibility. Open, closed, gender-specific, teen, or family meetings can have different rules.
  4. Verify before going. Use the official finder button on the card.
  5. Try more than one. The fellowship may fit even when one particular meeting does not.
Want broad support?

Look for SMART Recovery, Minnesota Recovery Connection All Recovery, Recovery Dharma, Refuge Recovery, NA, or Celebrate Recovery depending on the philosophy you prefer.

Compare recovery styles

Different meetings solve the “how” in different ways.

None of these categories is automatically better for everyone. The best fit is the one a person can engage with consistently and safely.

12

12-Step / Spiritual

Peer-led fellowships built around Steps, Traditions, sponsorship, shared experience, and a personally understood higher power.

Examples: AA, NA, CA, CMA, MA, GA, OA, CoDA.

↗

Secular / Skills-based

Focuses on practical behavior-change tools, self-management, motivation, coping skills, and peer support without a required spiritual framework.

Examples: SMART Recovery, LifeRing, SMART Family & Friends.

◌

Buddhist / Mindfulness

Uses meditation, mindfulness, ethical living, compassion, and investigation of craving and suffering as recovery practices.

Examples: Recovery Dharma, Refuge Recovery.

✦

Christian / Faith-based

Integrates Christian beliefs, scripture, prayer, community, and recovery principles.

Example: Celebrate Recovery.

◎

Indigenous / Cultural

Uses culturally grounded healing, circle process, community connection, spirituality, and Indigenous recovery teachings.

Example: Wellbriety / White Bison.

∞

All Pathways / Peer Recovery

Welcomes multiple definitions and methods of recovery rather than requiring one fellowship or philosophy.

Example: Minnesota Recovery Connection All Recovery.

First meeting guide

Common meeting words—plainly explained

Fellowships use slightly different definitions, so the official meeting listing always wins. These are practical general explanations.

What you usually do not have to do

  • You usually do not have to tell your whole story.
  • You usually do not have to speak if you are not ready.
  • You do not need to already “have recovery figured out.”
  • Many peer meetings do not require registration or payment.

Before you leave

  • Confirm day, time, address, and whether the meeting is in-person, online, or hybrid.
  • Check whether it is open or closed.
  • Check identity or age restrictions.
  • If virtual, look for passcodes or contact instructions on the official page.
Peer support is not emergency care.

If someone is in immediate danger or experiencing a life-threatening medical emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency department.

How this directory is maintained
